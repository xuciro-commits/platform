# ADR-0024: The ERP app — accounting, purchasing and inventory, production orders

**Status:** Accepted (2026-09-26, #115, the architecture gate of the ERP and the first proof of stage 7 in Platform.md §10.5). The owner delegated the gate ("u do go next dev step, I will test, u do u"), so D1 to D10 are decided as recommended; the owner may amend any of them before its batch is built. What is built is under "As built".

## Context

What exists:
- **The plant talks to an ERP it does not have.** Planned orders arrive by polling an external ERP (`equipment.go` `DeliverPlanned`, K8 poll) and are kept as claims; a supervisor releases a shop order against one; when its last SFC ends, the confirmation flow (`erp.go`) emits an effect to the ERP's endpoint and waits for its answer, and an agent proposes a correction when the ERP refuses. Locally, the ERP is a stand-in in `cmd/webhook-sink` (`POST /erp`).
- **What an ERP needs from the platform mostly exists:** records with lines and money (ADR-0016), lifecycles and approvals (ADR-0017), aggregates over records (ADR-0019), flows that call protocol actions with compensation (ADR-0020), protocols between apps (ADR-0011), and a scaffold (ADR-0023 6d).
- **One decision may write several records**: the rules of a decision return one function that puts every record it changes, applied only when the decision is accepted and replayed as one journal entry. Several changes of one app already stand or fall together; K4's open case (an atomic group across change records) is only needed across apps.

What is missing:
- **Money, posting and periods.** No app keeps accounts; nothing is double-entry; nothing refuses a change dated in a closed period.
- **Number sequences** (a stage 7 capability): record IDs are made by clients (`newId`), so nothing numbers documents without gaps, as accounting law in many countries requires.
- **Purchasing and inventory**, and **production orders** the MES executes, in the platform.

What the reference platforms do:

| | SAP S/4HANA | Odoo 19 |
|---|---|---|
| Journal entries | Universal journal (ACDOCA): one line table for every posting, balances derived | `account.move` with `account.move.line`; draft, then posted; balances from lines |
| Corrections | A posted document is never changed; reversal is a new document | Posted entries are locked; "Reverse" creates a reversal entry |
| Numbering | Number ranges per company code, document type and fiscal year | A sequence per journal and year; gaps are reported on the dashboard ([invoice sequence](https://www.odoo.com/documentation/19.0/applications/finance/accounting/customer_invoices/sequence.html)) |
| Periods | Posting periods opened and closed per company code | Lock dates; a hard lock is irreversible ([year-end closing](https://www.odoo.com/documentation/19.0/applications/finance/accounting/reporting/year_end.html)) |
| Inventory | Material documents; valuation at standard or moving average | Stock moves; valuation layers at standard, average or FIFO |
| MES integration | The ERP publishes production orders; the MES confirms yield, scrap and consumption through `API_PROD_ORDER_CONFIRMATION_2_SRV` or a BAPI ([SAP Help, production order integration](https://help.sap.com/docs/SAP_S4HANA_ON-PREMISE/21aead0c98bd4755abdacd91c99e3393/7d61c9ecd5754e8cb0e925639b5d8bb0.html); scope item IY5 in the cloud) | `mrp.production` and its moves in the same database |

Also ISA-95 / B2MML: the ERP sends a production schedule, the MES answers with production performance. Odoo's own pages could not be read from this environment (blocked); the table cites them from search results.

They agree:
- **Posted is final.** Corrections are new documents; periods close.
- **Every posting is a line**; balances are derived from lines, never kept as truth.
- **Numbers are per sequence and year**, taken when a document is posted.
- **The ERP owns production orders and receives confirmations**; the MES executes them.

Our constraints: replay never calls outside; the journal stays small enough to replay; rules stay typed code (ADR-0008); no new dependency; no domain vocabulary in `contract/`; the ERP stays thin (Platform.md §9 risk 3): its value is the pressure of money, posting, numbers, periods and production orders, not breadth.

## Design

1. **The ERP is an app** (`apps/erp`, Go package `erp`), scaffolded by `cmd/new-app`, knowing no other app. It declares its entity types, lifecycles and actions like any app; its dictionary is Chinese from the first commit.
2. **Accounting.** `erp.account` (code, name, kind: asset, liability, equity, income, expense). `erp.entry` (a journal entry: journal, date, reference, lines of account, debit, credit, text) is drafted, then **posted** by a decision whose rules check that debits equal credits, every account exists and is active, and the date's period is open. Posting takes the entry's number and writes one `erp.posting` record per line in the same decision; `erp.posting` is never edited. **Reversal** posts a new entry with the lines swapped and marks the first reversed. Balances, the trial balance and account statements are aggregates over postings (ADR-0019), never stored.
3. **Periods.** `erp.period` (a month) is open or closed; closing is a decision of the ERP's administrator, and so is reopening, audited like any decision. A posting dated in a closed period is refused.
4. **Number sequences are a platform capability.** An app declares sequences in its manifest (`platform.Sequence`: a name, a pattern such as `{year}/{n:5}`, reset yearly or never). Inside the function a decision applies, `Caller.Next(r, sequence, date)` takes the next number, so a refused decision takes none and numbers have no gaps. The counters are the host's state: rebuilt by replay, kept in snapshots. The ERP numbers journal entries per journal; the helpdesk numbers tickets, the second user.
5. **Purchasing and inventory.** `erp.partner` (suppliers and customers, the ERP's own), `erp.product` (unit, standard cost, the accounts it posts to, components for what the plant makes), `erp.purchase` (lines; draft, approved, received, billed; approval above an amount, through the work app), goods receipt as `erp.move` records (product, location, quantity in or out, reason) posted with an entry (inventory against goods received not invoiced), and the supplier's bill posted against it (goods received not invoiced against payables). On hand is an aggregate over moves.
6. **Production orders** (`erp.production`: product, quantity, due; draft, released, confirmed, closed). The protocol `production.orders/1` (`protocols/production`) is **provided by the ERP and consumed by the MES**:
   - read `orders`: released orders, the planned orders the plant may release shop orders against;
   - action `confirm`: yield, scrap and the shop order, decided by the ERP: accepted, it issues the components and receives the yield at standard cost (moves and one entry: work in progress, then finished goods, scrap to expense), and the order is confirmed; refused, the plant's flow gets the refusal at once;
   - event `released`: the plant tells the line's supervisors.
   The plant's confirmation flow acts through the protocol (ADR-0020 `Act.Protocol`), and the agent that corrects a refused confirmation reads the protocol's orders.
7. **The plant has one path to an ERP.** In a tenant with the ERP app, the protocol replaces polling and the confirmation effect. For an external ERP (SAP), the plant's connector and effect move into a small adapter app that provides the same protocol (7d); the plant keeps no ERP code of its own, and the sink's stand-in goes with it.
8. **Where it runs.** The plant host (`mes-server`, the rehearsal's plant company) composes the MES and the ERP; the rehearsal confirms an order through the protocol and reads the posting.
9. **Replay.** Every change is a decision of the ERP or the plant, journaled as today; numbers, balances and on-hand are rebuilt from it; `CheckReplay` covers each batch.

## Consequences

- The platform carries money: numbers, posting, periods and reversal are proven on real rules, not on a demo.
- Number sequences serve every app.
- The MES stops knowing any particular ERP: it consumes a protocol, and the ERP app or an adapter provides it.
- The ERP grows only when a capability needs its pressure.

## As built

### 7a: number sequences and accounting (#115)

- **Number sequences** (`platform/sequence.go`, host `sequence.go`): `Manifest.Sequences` declares `platform.Sequence{Name, Pattern, Yearly}` (`{n}`, `{n:5}`, `{year}`; checked at composition); `Caller.Next(r, sequence, date)` takes the next number in the function an accepted decision applies, refused in a probe or without a change record. The counters (`<app>/<sequence>/<year>`) are the host's, rebuilt by replay and kept in snapshots. The helpdesk numbers its tickets (`HD-2026-0001`), the ERP its entries per journal (`GJ/2026/00001`, `PJ/…`, `MJ/…`).
- **The ERP** (`apps/erp`, scaffolded by `cmd/new-app`): `erp.account` (the code is its ID; kind), `erp.entry` (journal, date, reference, lines of account, debit and credit; draft, posted, reversed), `erp.posting` (one per posted line, never edited), `erp.period` (a month, opened by `erp.period.open`, closed and reopened by the controller). Posting is the lifecycle's transition: its rules check two lines or more, each on an active account with one positive side, the tenant's currency, debits equal to credits, and an open period; then, in the same decision, it takes the number and puts the postings. A posted entry is neither edited nor archived. Reversal posts `<id>-R` with debits and credits swapped, on the payload's date or the entry's. The read `trial-balance` sums postings per account. Roles: accountant (entries) and controller (also the chart and periods).
- **The tenant's currency** (a finding): money had no default but the kit's `EUR`. The platform's setting `platform/currency` is the books' currency and the default of amounts people enter; apps read it as `Caller.Setting("platform/currency")`, `/v1/me` serves it, and the workspace sets the kit's default (`setCurrency`).
- **Lines in the kit** (a finding): the host describes a lines field's columns (`FieldInfo.Fields`; lines whose columns the kit cannot show stay read-only), the kit shows lines as a table on record pages and edits them as rows in forms (`Dialog wide`), lists leave them out, and generated forms offer the referred records as choices, for reference fields and lines' reference columns alike (ADR-0016's deferred picker).
- **Also found:** `Tenant.Member` looked members up by the subject they sign in as, not by ID; the ERP's development host found it. Pattern nesting was bounded at three levels, too few for a field listing five choices; it is eight. The decision toast is said in the reader's language.
- **Proven:** `TestBooks` (a refused posting takes no number; unbalanced, one-sided and closed-period postings refused; edit after posting refused; gapless numbers; reversal; close and reopen; the trial balance balances; `CheckReplay` with snapshots) and `TestChinese` in `apps/erp`; `TestHelpdeskTriage` (a refused ticket leaves no gap in ticket numbers, replayed); `scripts/verify.sh` composition and web. In the browser, in Chinese, on the ERP's development host: a controller drafted an entry with two lines, posted it as GJ/2026/00001 in CNY, and read a balanced trial balance.
- **Not yet:** production orders (7c), the adapter (7d); financial statements beyond the trial balance; the ERP in the Docker compose (it joins the plant host in 7c).

### 7b: purchasing and inventory (#115)

- **Declarations** (`apps/erp/server/purchasing.go`): `erp.partner` (supplier, customer or both), `erp.product` (its code is its ID; material or finished; unit; standard cost), `erp.purchase` (supplier, date, lines of product, quantity and unit price; draft, ordered, received, billed, canceled), `erp.move` (goods in or out, valued at standard cost, never edited). A buyer role places and receives orders; the chart gains 6403 Purchase price variance.
- **Placing an order** (`order`): its rules check a supplier, known products, positive quantities and prices in the tenant's currency, and compute the total; the order takes its number (`PO/2026/00001`) when placed. From the ERP's setting `approval-limit` (10 000 in whole units) a controller approves first, through the work app (ADR-0017): the level's condition reads the order's total.
- **Receiving** (`receive`): today's period must be open and the accounts must exist; in the same decision the goods move in and the receipt posts in the purchases journal: stock at standard cost, goods received not invoiced at the order's price, the difference to the price variance.
- **The supplier's bill** (`bill`, accountant or controller, with the supplier's invoice number): goods received not invoiced against payables.
- **On hand**: the read `on-hand` sums each product's moves and their value; stock moves also aggregate like any records.
- **UI** (`@pkg/erp`): purchase orders drafted with lines and moved along from their page, on hand, stock moves, products and partners. A record's history showed one decision's two changes to a record as one row twice; the kit now keys them apart.
- **Proven:** `TestPurchasing` (a customer is no supplier; an order under the limit is placed at once and one over it after the controller approves; a buyer books no bill; receipt and bill numbered in the purchases journal; on hand; stock, price variance and payables in the books, goods received not invoiced cleared; `CheckReplay`), `TestChinese`. In the browser, in Chinese, as the buyer on the development host: an order drafted, placed (PO/2026/00001) and received, and on hand showing 200 kg worth 1,000.00.
- **Not yet:** partial receipts and bills, returns, payments; the supplier's invoice number is entered only through the API, as record pages take a transition's payload from nobody (F-27).
